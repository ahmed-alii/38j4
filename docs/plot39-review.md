# Casa J-4: design review (rev 3)

A critic's pass over the plot 39 plans, the rules used, and what changed.

## Planning rules used

Drawn from standard residential planning practice, and from Pakistani practice where it differs.

1. **Zoning.** Guests stay at the front (porch, hall, drawing, dining, powder). Family life sits in the middle (lounges, kitchen). Bedrooms are upstairs or at the back. A guest never walks through a private room.
2. **Suite sequence: room, then wardrobe, then bath.** Enter the bedroom from the lounge or corridor (or through a small lobby or sitting room). The walk-in wardrobe sits between the bedroom and the bath, so it also blocks noise and steam. Never enter a bedroom through its wardrobe or its bath, and don't make the bath the only way to the wardrobe.
3. **Wet over wet.** First-floor baths stand directly over ground-floor baths or the laundry, so drains drop straight down and a leak can't stain a living room ceiling. Never put a bath over the kitchen's cooking area, the dining table, a bed or the drawing room.
4. **Every bath can breathe.** It has an outside wall with a window, or (on the top floor) a roof vent or skylight.
5. **Qibla.** The Qibla from Gujranwala is about 256°, roughly west. A WC must neither face it nor have its back to it, so WCs sit on a north–south line. A bed's feet should not point west.
6. **Beds.** The bed head goes against a solid wall: not under a window, not on a wall with a WC behind it, and not against the lounge TV wall. Leave 2′6″ on both sides and 3′ at the foot. Don't line the bed up with the door, so it isn't on show from the lounge.
7. **Circulation.** In Pakistani homes the upstairs TV lounge is the hub, and the bedrooms open off it. Long corridors waste area, so aim for under 10% of the floor.
8. **Doors.** Keep 3 ft clear on both sides of every door and opening. Doors must not clash. The lift must open onto a landing on every floor it serves.
9. **Climate (Gujranwala).** Bedrooms go south or east. West glass is shaded (shutters, balconies). Kitchens face north or east. Air should cross from one side of a room to the other where possible.
10. **Heavy loads go on walls.** Overhead water tanks sit on bath cores or stair walls, not in the middle of a lounge span.

## Rev 3 first floor

- **Upstairs lounge as the hub** (25′ × 12′, beside the gallery over the hall, with a skylight). Bedrooms 2 and 3, the store, the common bath and the master suite all open off it. Bedroom 4 is reached by the bridge across the hall. A glass door opens to a small balcony over the pool garden. The long corridor is gone.
- **Master suite:** lounge, then a sitting room, then through an arch into the bedroom (15′ × 12′8″, bed facing the garden balcony), then the walk-in wardrobe (7′8″ × 12′8″, his and hers), then the master bath. The bath sits **directly over the parents' bath** and has a south window. Its WC runs north–south.
- **Bedroom 3** gets its own bath, built **over the laundry** (dirty kitchen), with windows north and east. Its door sits between two wardrobes.
- **Common bath** for bedrooms 2 and 4 and guests, off the lounge, **over the parents' dressing room** beside their bath. It vents through the roof.
- **Bedrooms 2 and 4:** bed heads on solid walls, feet east or south. Bedroom 4 keeps its front balcony and gains a desk.
- **Store** off the lounge, against the north wall.
- **Lift** now opens onto the first-floor landing.
- **Water tanks** moved onto the walls of bedroom 3's bath.

Bath count is unchanged: 4 baths and a powder room for 5 bedrooms. The shared Jack-and-Jill bath became bedroom 3's own bath plus a common bath, because that is the only arrangement that puts every upstairs bath over a downstairs bath or the laundry.

First floor covered: 1,839 sq ft of 2,027 allowed (91%).

## Rev 3 ground floor

- The parents' bed moved to the south wall, between two windows, away from the lounge TV wall. A slim console now stands on the shared wall.
