# Plot 37 · Haveli J-4

A traditional South Punjab house on a compact footprint. Exposed brick with Multani blue tiles, jaalis, jharokas and roshandans; arched verandas at the front and back; a green belt of trees. Ground and first floor, with a tehkhana (basement) option. Plot 37 is identical to 38 and sits directly south of it.

## Brief (owner)

- **Size:** smaller house, more open space. Front lawn at the 15′9″ minimum; the space saved goes to the back garden.
- **Levels:** ground + first floor (the most the bye-laws allow), plus an optional basement.
- **Style:** traditional South Punjab, not Mediterranean or Spanish:
  - arched verandas, at the front and back but not all round;
  - brick with Multani blue tiles;
  - jaalis, jharokas and roshandans.
- **Climate:** passive cooling and a green belt of trees.
- **Kitchen:** one big closed kitchen (no open kitchen), with a pantry and a laundry.
- **Rooms:**
  - Drawing room opening onto the dining room, both off the entrance hall.
  - Family TV lounge, a study, and a staff room with its own bath.
  - Bedrooms: fewer bedrooms with full baths and smaller wardrobes (owner's choice). That gives three bedrooms, the master at the back.
- **Basement:** a tehkhana summer lounge.
- **Roof:** services only.
- **Cars:** a covered porch for two. No pool.

## Layout

**Ground floor:**
- **Arrival:** the car porch (two cars) sits at the front on the north side; the gate is at the north corner. The main door opens from the porch into the entrance hall, at the foot of the stair.
- **Guests:** entrance hall, then the drawing room (12′6″ × 17′), which opens onto the front veranda. A wide arch joins it to the dining room (9′3″ × 17′, 8 seats). The powder room is off the hall.
- **Family:** the family lounge (11′6″ × 17′) through an arch from the dining room, with glass doors to the rear veranda and garden.
- **Service:** the closed kitchen (11′11″ × 19′6″, island and breakfast table) at the back on the north-east: north and east light, no west sun. The dining room is three steps away across the hall. A pantry and a laundry sit next to the kitchen; the laundry has its own door to the side passage, and the kitchen a service door to the yard.
- **Staff:** staff room (10′11″ × 9′6″) with its own shower-WC, off the hall.

**First floor:**
- **Landing:** the stair arrives at a landing with a jharoka seat window over the porch.
- **Bedroom 2** (over the staff room), with its bath directly over the staff bath.
- **Bedroom 3** (over the kitchen), entered through a short lobby. Its bath is directly over the laundry.
- **Master suite** (at the back, over the family lounge): a sitting area by the door, then the bedroom (11′6″ × 23′ including the sitting area), then the walk-in wardrobe, then the master bath. The bath and wardrobe sit over the rear veranda, with a small terrace off the bedroom.
- **Upstairs lounge** (over the drawing room), opening onto the front terrace on the veranda roof.
- **Study** (over the dining room), with a day bed so it doubles as a guest room.

**Basement (option), the tehkhana:**
- A summer lounge (21′ × 27′).
- A guest room with its own bath.
- A store, and the plant room (UPS, pumps, filters).
- Roshandans just above the ground for air and light: the 3′6″ plinth leaves 2 ft of basement wall above grade.

## Planning rules applied

From the Casa J-4 design review ([plot39-review.md](plot39-review.md)):

- **Suite sequence:** room, then wardrobe, then bath. No room is entered through a wardrobe or a bath.
- **Wet stacking:** every first-floor bath stands over a ground-floor wet room (staff bath, laundry) or over the rear veranda. None is over a living room, the dining table, a bed or the kitchen.
- **Qibla:** WCs run north–south. No bed's feet point west.
- **Kitchen:** closed, beside the dining room, on its own service route (laundry, pantry, service yard, side passage). The staff reach it without passing through a living room.

## Passive cooling

1. **Thick walls:** 13½″ cavity brick outer walls. The air gap and the brick's mass slow the afternoon heat.
2. **High ceilings and roshandans:** 11′6″ on the ground floor. High vents let the hottest air out near the ceiling.
3. **Stair as a thermal chimney:** the stairwell rises into a brick wind tower (badgir) with open jaali arches on all four sides. Hot air rises up the stair and out of the tower, drawing cooler air in through the north windows and the shaded verandas.
4. **The west face is shaded:** the front veranda, jharokas and jaali screens. The drawing room's west doors sit 8 ft back under the veranda roof. There is little bare west glass.
5. **Deep verandas:** east (morning sun only) and west. 2 ft chhajjas (sunshades) on the south and west of the first floor, inside the 2 ft that may project into clear spaces.
6. **Light roof:** light terracotta roof tiles over insulation, and the roof shaded by the solar panels (26 panels, about 14 kW, which also qualifies for the bye-law 22(b) solar relief).
7. **Green belt:**
   - trees along the rear and south boundaries (the south side is the hot side) and two in the front lawn;
   - a hedge screening the service yard;
   - climbers on the south wall;
   - a grape-vine pergola for sitting out.
8. **Tehkhana:** the basement stays close to earth temperature (about 24 °C) through the summer.
